**Words used here.** A *hold* is money set aside before a model call and settled to its cost afterwards; a call is *admitted* when the gateway lets it through to the provider. The *cap* is a project's spending limit. A *stand-in provider* is a fake model provider used in tests; nothing real is called or spent. The *key* is the secret that lets the builder call the provider. A *proof row* (B1 to B19) is one line of the design note's table of things that must be shown. The *sweep* is the gateway's pass, every 30 seconds, that marks holds past their deadline. A *lens* is the one angle a reviewer is told to read from. A *blocking* finding stops a merge; a *should-fix* point is to be fixed but does not stop one; a *note* is smaller. A finding is *confirmed* when one of the two agents told to show it wrong could not. A *re-reader* reads the answers to an earlier round. A *control* is a test run once with its fix removed, to show that the test can fail. The *review bot* is an automated reviewer on GitHub that reads a pull request's latest commit.

## What the reviews do not show

Every run was against a stand-in provider: none shows how the real provider counts, bills or fails (package 2.3). The cap limits admission, not the bill: a bill above its hold is charged in full and can take spend above the cap, and the fault it raises stops nothing until package 1.4 (known gaps 41 and 62). Proof row B16 shows only that a margin is applied, not that it is large enough. No review is a statement that the cap is secure or ready for real spending.
